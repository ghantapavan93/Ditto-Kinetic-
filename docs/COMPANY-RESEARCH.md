## The product, as the founders describe it

- Original surface is **iMessage, not an app**. You text a number with a code and talk
  to an AI chatbot. Onboarding is conversational: biographical info, personality,
  interests, dating preferences. Some users send celebrity-crush photos as taste signal.
- Matching optimises for **one thing: getting two people on a real date.** No browsing,
  no inbox, no conversation management. Ditto plans one date at a time — person, time,
  and place — and you say yes or no.
- Matches drop **Wednesdays at 7pm**. This is the "drop day" the web onboarding refers to.
- Matching is on **personality, not shared hobbies**. Wang's example: a guy into rock
  climbing and skydiving and a girl into hip hop and skateboarding are a match because
  both are adventurous and individualistic.
- Verification is **.edu email**, which is also their stated blocker for expanding
  beyond campuses.

## Read: the three openings

Things that are true, public, and unresolved — i.e. what a pitch can actually grip.

**1. The 20% problem.**
Their entire thesis is "we get you on a real date." Four out of five matched pairs
don't go. That is the single number standing between the promise and the product, and
they publish it. Anything that credibly moves match-to-date conversion is aimed at the
centre of the company.

**2. The web app is new and unfinished.**
Ditto started as a text-message bot. The `app.ditto.ai/join` flow is them building a
real front end for the first time, with 12 people and no dedicated design team visible.
Front-end quality is an *active, open* problem there — not a solved one they'd be
defensive about.

**3. Identity breaks at the campus boundary.**
`.edu` verification works exactly as long as they stay in college. They've said out
loud that they intend to leave. There is no announced answer for what replaces it.
