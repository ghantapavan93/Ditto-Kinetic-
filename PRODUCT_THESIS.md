# PRODUCT THESIS

## The right person can still get the wrong first date.

Ditto solved the hard half. It removed the deck, it picks one person, it delivers
one introduction on Wednesday at 7pm. The matching is not the problem.

Then it books a coffee.

**20% of matched pairs actually go on the date.** That number is Ditto's own,
and it is the distance between "we found the right person" and "two people met."
Four out of five introductions do not survive contact with a time and a place.

The instinct is to read that as a matching failure and score people harder. It
isn't. Two people who would work on a walk after a show will still fail across a
café table at 6pm, and no amount of additional compatibility signal fixes that,
because the thing that failed was not the pair.

---

## A match is a pair under conditions.

The ranking object should not be:

```
Person A × Person B
```

It should be:

```
Person A × Person B × context
```

where context is time, setting, energy, social pressure, activity, travel
friction, availability, safety, novelty, conversation runway, and how much the
system still doesn't know.

In FIRST SCENE, `pairSignal` is one field out of eleven — and it is **constant
across all six candidate openings**. The same two people are exactly as
compatible in every row of the table. Everything that separates the best opening
from the worst one is context.

That is the whole argument, and you can see it as a flat line in the decision
view.

---

## Two consequences

**1. Bad context should feel wrong before it is explained.**
The interface physically resists openings that don't work: the photographs stay
apart, their rotations refuse to align, the connection line hangs slack, objects
never settle. When the right opening arrives, everything snaps into one
composition. Nobody has to read a percentage to know which one it is.

**2. The system should then get out of the way.**
Ditto's manifesto says technology should "make the introduction, then get out of
the way." So the richest screen in this piece deletes itself: the reasoning goes,
the artifacts go, the dial goes, the 3D world goes, the photographs go, and what
is left is one text message and then one line.

> go have a real life.

Complexity before the introduction. Simplicity after it. The motion *is* the
philosophy — it is not illustrating it.

---

## What this is not arguing

Not that Ditto's matching is wrong. It is arguing the opposite: the matching is
good enough that the remaining loss is downstream of it.

Not that a percentage would help. The consumer surface never shows a score,
because "87% compatible" is a worse sentence than "they don't need more
compatibility, they need less pressure."

Not that the system is certain. Every scene carries a `what we don't know yet`
note that never resolves, the winning scene's uncertainty is the highest of the
top three, and post-date learning produces a *hypothesis*, struck through against
the old one, not an update.

---

## The falsifiable version

If the thesis is right, the same six rooms scored for a different pair should
produce a different winner — otherwise this is a cinematic, not a system.

Press **other pair**. Priya has twelve-hour clinical shifts and doesn't need an
activity to open up; Theo finds activities a way of avoiding the conversation.
Same engine, same weights, same six rooms.

Coffee wins. The post-show walk comes last.
